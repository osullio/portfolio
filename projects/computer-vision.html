Overview

Surveillance systems need to flag two kinds of change: a bag that's been left behind, and something that's been taken. I wrote an OpenCV/C++ program that finds both in video, labels each detection as abandoned or removed, and scores itself against hand-labelled ground truth.

How it works

  1. Background subtraction. A Gaussian Mixture Model (BackgroundSubtractorMOG2) separates foreground from background.
  2. Motion mask. Differencing each frame against one from five frames earlier separates moving foreground from static foreground.
  3. Persistence. Pixels that stay foreground without moving build up a count. A region that stays still for about 6 seconds becomes a candidate.
  4. Filtering & tracking. Candidates are filtered by area (150–3000 px) and aspect ratio, tracked across frames, merged when they overlap, and confirmed after 15 consistent frames.
  5. Classification. Edges in the current frame are compared with a baseline of the empty scene. New edges mean an object was abandoned. Edges that have gone missing mean one was removed.

Evaluation

Each detection is matched to ground truth when IoU ≥ 0.5 and it falls within ±120 frames. The program reports precision, recall and F1 for each video and overall, plus classification accuracy.
